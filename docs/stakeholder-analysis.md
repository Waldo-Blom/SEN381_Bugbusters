## Stakeholder analysis

The project consists of 5 different identified stakeholders, each of them having different needs and ways in which they will interact with the digital platform. Some stakeholders might have conflicts or competing expectations. Below is the breakdown for each stakeholder:

### 1. Product owner 

This is the person who wants the system in the first place. They are the “community-focused organization ” that identified the problem. Their responsibility is to ensure that they clearly communicate their needs and expectations for the system that needs to be developed.

They do not submit actual issues. They review the system during the milestones to ensure that it meets the expectations and aligns with the business needs and functional requirements.

Conflict / competing expectation: The Project owner wants the digital platform to have as much functionality as possible within the fixed budget and schedule. This could clash with Development teams need for a stable scope and enough time to build a quality product that is also maintainable. If the scope is not controlled properly, this could lead to scope creep. That is why scope will be controlled and any changes in scope will not just be accepted, they will first be considered against the baseline scope identified and only in the event that scope change is deemed absolutely necessary, it will accepted be properly documented.


### 2. Development team (Waldo, Christian and Marco – “Bugbusters”) 

This is us (Waldo, Christian and Marco) the “Bugbusters” team who are actually developing the project based on the identified requirements. Our responsibility is to ensure that we clearly understand the business need in order to identify the requirements of the project . But also, to ensure that we build a system that is maintainable, reliable and secure.

We will test the system to ensure that it works correctly. We will not add additional items to the scope before properly considering the implications of doing so, also in the event that the scope changes we will properly document the decision and the change.

Conflict / competing expectation: We must balance the speed at which we develop the application with the quality and maintainability of the end product. With a fixed schedule and free tier constraints we must choose quality and sustainability over just implementing something that works and gets the job done.

### 3. Requester 

This is the person who experiences the actual problem i.e a fault, security concern, lost property, or any other operational request.

Their responsibility is to provide accurate and clear information about the issue they are experiencing, and to label the issue correctly according to the request category. They should also respond to service staff when asked for more information about their issue.

Their core need is to stay updated on their request. They want as much visibility as possible when it comes to their logged request, i.e. knowing when it was received, assigned, in progress and resolved. They want a system that is easy to use and easy to interact with, they don’t want something is complex and difficult to use.

Conflict / competing expectation: Requesters want a experience that is simple and quick however the staff and management need sufficient information to be able to resolve the issue that the requester is experiencing. These needs clash and need to be balanced, though the digital platform that will be developed.

### 4. Management 
Note: Each department manager (e.g., IT Manager, Facilities Manager) is not a separate stakeholder; Management overall is treated as a single stakeholder group.

Management oversees their specific category (i.e. IT sees IT related requests, Facilities Manger sees the requests associated with facility requests) of requests and monitors activity through a dashboard (see FR-301 of the functional requirements section), which thereby provides the visibility of active and closed requests. In order to improve operational efficiency and reduce management workload, staff may self-assign tasks. However, Management still has the final authority to approve, override, or redirect requests as needed.

Their responsibility is to ensure that the issues assigned to their department is resolved within the expected time frame. Also, to authorize release of contact information of the Requesters to the staff only when needed, for example to get further context about an issue when it was not clearly stated (Scope item nr. 9 & out of scope item 2).

Conflict / competing expectation: Management want detailed reporting and accountability of their staff (who did what, when etc.). However, staff want minimal data entry and an interface that is easy and allows for fast data entry. Privacy of the requesters must also be ensured, information must only be provided when absolutely necessary as the requesters value their privacy of their data but at the same time if they are not specific enough about their issue, they will need to be contacted for further clarification.

### 5. Staff 
Note: Each staff member is not a separate stakeholder; The staff overall is treated as a single stakeholder group.

The staff are the people who are actually assigned to investigate, handle and resolve the service requests made by the requester.

Their primary responsibility is to accept and claim ownership of the issue they are assigned to, including status updates, resolution details and contacting the requester in the event there is clarification needed (Scope item nr. 9 & out of scope item 2).

Their core need is to clearly see the issues that they are assigned to, and to be able to update the status of the requests they are working on. They want an interface that is easy to interact with and that requires minimal clicks and fast loading time (As they are going to be using this system a lot).

Conflict / competing expectation: Staff may resist to use this newly proposed solution in the event that it is slower than the current informal methods (WhatsApp, email, telephone calls etc.). In order to ensure that they will want to use the system, we must make the system as easy as possible to use, while still ensuring management has detailed reporting and accountability of their staff (who did what, when etc.).
